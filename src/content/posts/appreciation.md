While I was unsurprised by the fact that the highest rates of anxiety and depression were found in graduate students, I was shocked by the difference in rates of anxiety and depression among those in the LGBTQ+ community. I knew there would be a difference, but I didn’t expect it to be that large. In hindsight, looking at the researchers I have known who were LGBTQ+ or questioning, it introduced a massive amount of stress into day to day activities. Several former lab mates of mine are transitioning, and a gargantuan effort is required to change your name on previously published papers, or on your orchid, or on posters. The decision about when and where to come out is complicated when you’re in a smaller team – individual opinions can matter greatly. There are very few people that direct your life track like your supervisor, so I can imagine the anxiety around whether or not to come out to them must massively impact your life. That, in addition to the financial stress grad students feel, can make transition very difficult. With LGBTQ+ students being the only demographic that are more likely to leave the field, we need to take another look at the environment we create for these students. 
After 12 minutes of complete blackout, I found myself back in my seat. I came to and realized: it went well! I got a laugh out of the audience at the very beginning that allowed me to loosen up and relax through the rest of it. Did I make some small mistakes while speaking? Yes. Was I perfectly engaging every corner of the room? No.
